# Step 1: Decide the domain name

For example:
abcconsulting.com
abctax.in
johnsharma.in

Don't buy it under your account. Buy it under the client's account.

It means the account used to purchase the domain should belong to the client, not you.

For example, suppose your client is Rahul.

❌ Don't do this

You log into your Namecheap account:

Your Namecheap account
└── rahultax.com

Even if Rahul pays you, the domain legally belongs to your account. If you disappear, forget your password, or stop working together, Rahul can't manage his own website.

✅ Do this instead

Ask Rahul to create a Namecheap account (or Cloudflare/Hostinger account).

Rahul's Namecheap account
└── rahultax.com

Then:

Rahul pays for the domain (or you can use your card if he reimburses you).
The domain is registered in Rahul's account.
Rahul owns it from day one.

What if the client is not technical?

This is very common.

You can simply ask them to:

Create a Namecheap account using their email.
Share the login with you temporarily or
Join a screen-sharing call while you purchase the domain.

Once the domain is bought, they can change the password if they want.

Real-world example

Suppose your client's business is ABC Tax Consultants.

Client creates:

Namecheap account → abc.tax@gmail.com (or their personal email initially)

You log in to their account and buy:

abctax.com

Later you:

configure DNS
connect Vercel
create business email
set up GitHub

But everything is owned by the client.

# Step 2: Buy the domain

Use a registrar such as:

Namecheap
Cloudflare Registrar
Hostinger
BigRock

After this, the client owns the domain.

# Step 3: Create the business email

Now you have the domain, you can create an email like:

info@abcconsulting.com
contact@abcconsulting.com
hello@abcconsulting.com

You have two common choices:

Zoho Mail (free for small businesses)
Google Workspace (paid, Gmail interface)

During setup, they'll ask you to verify ownership of the domain by adding a DNS record. Since you already own the domain, this is straightforward.

# Step 4: Create the remaining accounts using that email
Once contact@abcconsulting.com exists, use it to create:

GitHub
Vercel
Google Analytics
Google Business Profile
Any future business services

This keeps everything tied to the business rather than the client's personal Gmail.

Why this order?
```
Choose domain
      ↓
Buy domain
      ↓
Create business email
      ↓
Create GitHub
      ↓
Create Vercel
      ↓
Deploy website
      ↓
Configure DNS
      ↓
Go live
```
The only small exception is if the client wants Google Workspace. They'll initially sign up using a temporary personal email, then after domain verification Google creates the business mailbox (contact@domain.com). That's a normal part of the setup process.

So yes—buy the domain first, then create the business email, then use that business email for all the other business accounts. This is the cleanest and most professional workflow.
